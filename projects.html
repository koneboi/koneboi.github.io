DaT Parkinson's Challenge

A 13-member Bayesian ensemble classifying DaT scans as normal vs abnormal using 2D MIP-CNN + 3D volume CNNs and Platt-scaled z-score averaging.

Ensemble Architecture

Each DaT NIfTI scan is anatomy-aligned (brain center-of-mass crop), projected to MIPs or resampled to a 3D grid, then processed by a member-specific model with flip-TTA. Member logits are z-normalized and Platt-calibrated.

Technical Notes

Anatomy-Aligned Preprocessing

Instead of center-cropping the scanner field of view (which varies 135–630 mm across institutions), each scan is centered on the brain center of mass and cropped to a fixed physical cube (120–200 mm). This acts as a cheap registration, landing the striatum in a consistent position across scanners and hospital sites.

Log-Loss Optimised Ensemble

Leaderboard metric is log loss, so raw scores are irrelevant — only calibrated probabilities matter. Each member's logits are z-normalized with honest single-fold OOF statistics, averaged across members, then passed through a Platt (logistic) fit on the same OOF data to produce a proper probability.

The Calibration Bug That Was Fixed

Early submissions scored 0.30–0.34 due to a flip-dimension mismatch: OOF calibration flipped along dims=[2,1] (height + channel axis), while the runtime flipped along dims=[3,2] (spatial width + height). This placed calibration on a logit distribution (std ∼3.14) that the runtime never produces (std ∼4.82), making Platt severely overconfident. Fixing OOF to use the exact runtime flip dims recovered ∼0.095 of log loss.

Competition Context

Hosted by the French Society of Nuclear Medicine (SFMN), Health Data Hub and GaelO under France 2030. Dataset: multicenter DaT scans from 10 French hospital centres. 472 registered, 361 scored on private test. Metric: log loss (lower = better).
